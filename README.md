### 📄 Abstract
Parkinson's disease (PD) is associated with alterations in cortical dynamics that classical spectral measures, such as band power, capture only partially. Information-theory quantifiers built on the Bandt–Pompe symbolic approach offer a complementary, model-free description of the temporal structure of EEG signals, sensitive to the non-stationary, bout-like nature of brain activity that power spectra tend to average out. We analyzed eyes-open resting-state EEG from PD patients (n = 100) and age-matched healthy controls (n = 49). Signals were decomposed into the canonical frequency bands (delta, theta, alpha, beta and gamma). For each band, we estimated four information-theory metrics: Shannon entropy, Martín–Plastino–Rosso statistical complexity (C), Fisher information, and permutation entropy, both in the full signal and within bouts (Within-Bout). PD patients and controls differed significantly in these metrics, with the largest effects in the alpha band, where a significant reduction in all four metrics was observed (p < 0.05). In the theta band, a significant increase in bout dynamics was observed (higher number of bouts, longer duration, higher amplitude and cycles; p < 0.05). Moreover, Theta correlated with clinical progression: its information-theory metrics correlated positively with MOCA (rho ≈ 0.30, p < 0.003), and Fisher information correlated negatively with UPDRS (rho = -0.23, p = 0.025). The beta band showed the classical finding of power hypoactivity (45 significant electrodes, p < 0.05), but no significant differences in information-theory metrics or correlation with clinical scales. These results show that band-resolved entropy and complexity quantifiers capture differences in resting-state EEG dynamics between PD patients and controls, and that Theta is a biomarker of clinical progression (cognition and motor function), complementing conventional spectral and bout-based markers.
